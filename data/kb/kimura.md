# Kimura from Closed Guard

## Position
Bottom player in closed guard. Gi or no-gi. Also available from side control and half guard.

## Goal
Apply a figure-four grip on the opponent's arm and rotate the shoulder (shoulder lock).

## Steps
1. Wait for the opponent to post a hand on the mat, or push their wrist to the mat beside your hip.
2. Grab that wrist with the hand on the same side.
3. Open the guard, sit up and reach your other arm over the opponent's shoulder, then grab your own wrist (figure-four grip).
4. Fall back to the mat, shrimp toward the side of the attacked arm and put your leg over the opponent's back to stop them from rolling.
5. Keep the elbow bent at about 90 degrees and rotate the hand toward the opponent's back slowly until they tap.

## Common mistakes
- Letting the opponent straighten the arm: keep the elbow at 90 degrees.
- Not shrimping to the side: without the angle there is no rotation.
- Forgetting to control the back with the leg: the opponent rolls out.

## Defenses
- Grab your own belt or gi to block the rotation.
- Keep the elbow tight to the body and posture up before the figure four is locked.

## Safety
Shoulder locks damage ligaments quickly. Rotate slowly, release at the first tap, tap early when caught, and train under supervision.
